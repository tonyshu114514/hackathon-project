        Disclosures & methodology

        Prototype for screening only. Not legal, zoning or real estate advice. Rankings use ASSUMPTION weights; confirm every site with the city and a qualified professional before acting on it.

        How a site is scored

        Each site earns points for open land, listed lower-income capacity, size, transit access, a high-opportunity location and a tax-credit area, and loses points for a FEMA flood zone, fire hazard zones, a landslide zone, a cleanup site on or near the parcel and a high pollution burden. The score is shown as a share of the points attainable with the data the site has, so a missing term is not a penalty, and that share sets the 1 to 5 stars. Weights, cutoffs and star bands are team assumptions, listed below and adjustable in the left panel.

        Likely already used

        A site is marked likely used when the city reported permit activity on its APN to HCD since 2021 (graded strong for a permit or completion, moderate for an entitlement) or a building on it is dated 2021 or later. ADU-only activity is a caution, not a removal. Likely-used sites are never ranked. Cities type APNs by hand, so a miss is not proof of no activity and a hit can be wrong.

        Development pathway screens

        SB 9, SB 4, AB 2011 and SB 684 / SB 1123 are screened with signals taken from HCD's Housing Law Fact Sheets (April 2026) against the layers loaded: zoning family, lot size, fire, flood and cleanup data, public ownership and site activity. A law reads No when a checkable test fails and Unknown otherwise. It never reads "yes": ownership, tenancy, historic status, street width, industrial neighbours and the other protected lands in Gov. Code § 65913.4(a)(6) are in no public dataset we load. Each test's sentence and source is on the site's Pathways tab.

        What this does not check

        • Ownership and willingness to sell. Parcel data has no owner field here.
        • Which state law (SB 9, SB 684, AB 2011, SB 4) applies. The pathway screens are signals from HCD fact sheets, not each law's legal tests.
        • Contamination beyond DTSC EnviroStor (State Water Board GeoTracker leak sites are not loaded), and any Phase I review.
        • Water and sewer capacity, and city objective standards.
        • APR rows are typed by city staff. An APN that does not match is not proof of no activity.

        Surplus Land Act notices

        Public agencies' notices come from HCD's statewide list. Nearly all are archived, meaning the 60-day window has passed; the land may still be available, so the agency is the one to ask. Where no county parcel matches the APN, the outline is an approximate circle of the listed acreage (ASSUMPTION) and is marked as such.

        Map and basemap

        The default basemap is USGS The National Map (Topo), a U.S. government service in the public domain that needs no API key. USGS Imagery, Esri streets and Esri dark gray (Esri terms, attribution shown) are in the layers menu, and OpenStreetMap is offered when the page is served over the web: its volunteer servers block requests without a Referer, which includes any page opened as a local file. If the active basemap's tiles fail, the map falls back to the next one and the status pill on the map says so. Inside Claude artifacts no basemap loads and the drawn layers give the geography.
